# Employee Attrition Analysis Using Python

## Project Overview

This project analyses employee attrition data to identify patterns and factors associated with employees leaving an organisation.

The analysis was performed using Python and focuses on factors such as overtime, monthly income, age, job satisfaction, department, job role and years at the company.

## Tools Used

- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Jupyter Notebook
- Visual Studio Code

## Dataset

The dataset contains 1,470 employee records and 35 variables relating to employee demographics, job characteristics and employment history.

The target variable is **Attrition**, which indicates whether an employee left the company.

## Data Quality

Before performing the analysis, the dataset was checked for:

- Missing values
- Duplicate records
- Data types
- Target variable distribution

The dataset contained **0 missing values** and **0 duplicate records**.

## Key Findings

- Overall employee attrition rate: **16.12%**
- Employees working overtime had an attrition rate of **30.53%**, compared with **10.44%** for employees who did not work overtime.
- Employees who left earned an average monthly income of **4,787.09**, compared with **6,832.74** for employees who stayed.
- Employees who left were younger on average (**33.61 years**) than employees who stayed (**37.56 years**).
- Average job satisfaction was lower among employees who left (**2.47**) compared with employees who stayed (**2.78**).
- The **Sales department** had the highest departmental attrition rate at **20.63%**.
- **Sales Representatives** had the highest job-role attrition rate at **39.76%**.
- Employees who left had an average tenure of **5.13 years**, compared with **7.37 years** for employees who stayed.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the organisation could:

1. Review overtime workloads and employee work-life balance.
2. Prioritise retention initiatives for Sales Representatives.
3. Review compensation and career progression opportunities.
4. Strengthen onboarding and retention initiatives for employees with shorter tenure.
5. Monitor employee satisfaction through regular engagement surveys and manager check-ins.

## Conclusion

The analysis identified several characteristics associated with employee attrition, particularly overtime, job role, income, age, job satisfaction and tenure.

Overtime and Sales Representative roles emerged as areas that may warrant particular attention.

The findings represent associations and do not establish that these factors directly cause employee attrition.
